Book 1 · about 40 h · needs: none

Mathematics for Quantum Engineering

Every piece of mathematics the rest of the series uses, built from school algebra: complex numbers, vector spaces and Dirac notation, eigenvalues and matrix exponentials, tensor products, differential equations, Fourier analysis, probability and numerical optimisation.

8 chapters
  1. Complex numbers, the exponential and phasors
  2. Vector spaces, inner products and Dirac notation
  3. Linear operators, eigenvalues and the matrix exponential
  4. Tensor products and composite systems
  5. Differential equations and linear dynamics
  6. Fourier analysis, linear systems and spectra
  7. Probability, statistics and inference
  8. Numerical methods and optimisation

Book 2 · about 45 h · needs: Book 1

Classical Physics for Quantum Engineers

The classical physics that quantum hardware is built from: least action and Hamiltonians, oscillators and normal modes, Maxwell's equations, capacitance and inductance matrices, transmission lines and resonators, optical waveguides, and the thermodynamics of noise.

8 chapters
  1. From Newton to Lagrange: the principle of least action
  2. Hamiltonian mechanics and phase space
  3. Oscillators: damping, driving, coupling and nonlinearity
  4. Electrostatics, magnetostatics and the capacitance matrix
  5. Maxwell's equations, waves and materials
  6. Transmission lines, resonators and microwave networks
  7. Optics, waveguides and interferometers
  8. Thermal physics, noise and cryogenics

Book 3 · about 60 h · needs: Book 1, Book 2

Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics from the qubit up: postulates, Schrödinger dynamics, tunnelling, the harmonic oscillator, spin and Rabi oscillations, perturbation theory, density matrices and entanglement, decoherence and the Lindblad equation, and second quantisation.

9 chapters
  1. Why quantum mechanics: experiments and the qubit
  2. States, observables and measurement
  3. Dynamics, wavefunctions and tunnelling
  4. The quantum harmonic oscillator
  5. Spin, two-level systems and Rabi oscillations
  6. Approximation methods: perturbation theory and effective Hamiltonians
  7. Density matrices, composite systems and entanglement
  8. Open quantum systems and decoherence
  9. Identical particles and second quantisation

Book 4 · about 50 h · needs: Book 3

Quantum Information and Computation

What a quantum computer computes and why it is fragile: the circuit model, teleportation, Grover and Shor, quantum channels, fidelity and randomised benchmarking, stabiliser codes, the surface code and its threshold, and how programs become pulses.

8 chapters
  1. Qubits, gates and circuits
  2. Entanglement as a resource: no-cloning, teleportation and networks
  3. Quantum algorithms I: interference, oracles and search
  4. Quantum algorithms II: Fourier transform, phase estimation, factoring and simulation
  5. Noise, fidelity and benchmarking
  6. Quantum error correction I: codes and stabilisers
  7. Quantum error correction II: the surface code and fault tolerance
  8. From program to pulses: the quantum computing stack

Book 5 · about 45 h · needs: Book 2, Book 3, Book 4

Quantum Photonics

Light as a quantum system and as a qubit: field quantisation, coherent and squeezed states, beam splitters and Hong–Ou–Mandel interference, cavity QED, photon sources and detectors, integrated photonic circuits, and loss-tolerant photonic quantum computing.

7 chapters
  1. Quantising the electromagnetic field
  2. Quantum states of light
  3. Linear optics and two-photon interference
  4. Light–matter interaction and cavity QED
  5. Single-photon sources and detectors
  6. Integrated quantum photonics: platforms, components and fabrication
  7. Photonic quantum computing: from KLM to fusion and GKP

Book 6 · about 55 h · needs: Book 2, Book 3, Book 4, Book 5

Quantum Hardware: Superconducting Circuits and Other Qubits

How physical qubits are built: superconductivity and the Josephson junction, quantising a circuit, the transmon and fluxonium, dispersive readout, single- and two-qubit gates, where coherence is lost, and the trapped-ion, neutral-atom and spin-qubit alternatives.
