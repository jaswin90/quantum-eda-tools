Why this series

The review From Laboratory to Foundry argues that quantum processors are now manufactured products, and that what they lack is the design automation that made classical chips predictable: process design kits, extraction with error bars, statistical yield models and sign-off. Reading it, and working on its open problems, needs three kinds of knowledge at once: the physics of quantum devices, the engineering of microwave and photonic circuits and of fabrication, and the mathematics of statistics and optimisation. Few people are taught all three. This series teaches them in order, from first principles, with the review as its destination.

Each book is self-contained and can be read on its own, but they are designed as one course. Every chapter opens with its goals, derives what it uses rather than quoting it, works examples drawn from real devices, ends with exercises that have full solutions, and closes with a box labelled Toward quantum EDA that says where the chapter's ideas reappear in design tools and in the review. References between books and into the review are links, so a reader of the review can follow any equation back to where it is taught.

Reading order

The full course is about {{TOTALHOURS}} hours of study, roughly a semester of full-time work or a year part-time. Three shorter routes suit readers who already know part of the material:

  • Physicist's route (quantum mechanics already known): skim Books 1 and 3, read Book 2 Chapters 1–2 and 4–8, Book 4 Chapters 5–8, then Books 6 and 7 in full.
  • Engineer's route (circuits and EM already known): Book 1 Chapters 2–4 and 7–8, Book 3 in full, Book 4 in full, Book 6, then Book 7 Chapters 4, 6 and 7.
  • Photonics route: Book 1 Chapters 1–4, Book 2 Chapters 5 and 7, Book 3 Chapters 1–5 and 7–9, Book 4 Chapters 1–2 and 5–7, Book 5 in full, Book 7 Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 6.

Capstone

From Laboratory to Foundry: Design Automation for Quantum Processors

The review and research agenda that the series prepares for: the industrial landscape, the physics and mathematics of each step from qubit design to calibrated operation, the existing tools, and seven open problems. Book 7, Chapter 7 maps each of its sections back to the series.

Conventions

  • Units: SI throughout. Frequencies "in GHz" mean \(\omega/2\pi\). Some quantum chapters set \(\hbar = 1\) and say so.
  • Time dependence: physics chapters use \(\ee^{-\ii\omega t}\); microwave-engineering chapters use \(\ee^{+j\omega t}\) (written with \(\ii\)) and say so. Results differ by complex conjugation (@b1:sec-m-complex-phasor).
  • Qubit labels: \(\ket0\) is the ground state. Pauli \(Z\ket0 = +\ket0\). Tensor products list the first qubit first (the NumPy kron order).
  • Cross-references: "Book 3 §5.2" links to that section; "Lab to Foundry §4.2" links into the review.
  • Citations: each book has its own numbered reference list of textbooks and papers. Entries marked "verify details" were compiled from memory and should be checked before being cited elsewhere.
