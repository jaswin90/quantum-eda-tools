How to use this book

A quantum processor is a classical object for almost all of its design. Its metal patterns are drawn by lithography, its fields obey Maxwell's equations, its signals travel down transmission lines, its heat flows to a refrigerator, and its noise comes from resistors and amplifiers. Only at the very end does one "quantise" the result. That last step is mechanical once the classical description is right: write the energy as a Lagrangian, convert it to a Hamiltonian, and replace Poisson brackets by commutators. This book builds the classical description and leaves the system ready to be quantised in Books 3, 5 and 6.

Chapters 1–3 develop mechanics in the form quantum theory needs: least action, Lagrangians, Hamiltonians and oscillators. Chapters 4–6 develop electromagnetism from charges to microwave circuits, ending with the transmission-line resonators that are the workhorses of superconducting quantum hardware. Chapter 7 does the same for light in optical waveguides, which is the starting point of Book 5 on photonics. Chapter 8 covers thermal physics and noise, which decide why quantum hardware has to be cold and how much noise reaches a qubit.

Prerequisites are the first six chapters of Book 1. The chapters use SI units throughout; useful constants are \(e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\) C, \(h = 6.626\times10^{-34}\) J s, \(\hbar = h/2\pi\), \(k_B = 1.381\times10^{-23}\) J/K, \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}\) F/m and \(\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\) H/m (to the precision used here).

Every chapter follows the same pattern: a "big picture" box saying why the topic matters for quantum hardware, the physics built up from a worked example, a short "Try it in Python" section you can run and modify, a box connecting the chapter to design automation, a list of key points, and exercises with solutions. If a chapter feels heavy, read the big picture, the worked examples and the key points first, then come back for the derivations.

Words and symbols you will meet

\(L = T - V\)
The Lagrangian: kinetic minus potential energy. Its time integral is the action.
\(q\), \(p\)
A generalised coordinate and its conjugate momentum, \(p = \partial L/\partial\dot q\).
\(H\)
The Hamiltonian: the energy written as a function of \(q\) and \(p\). Quantum mechanics starts from it.
\(\{f,g\}\)
Poisson bracket; becomes the commutator \([\hat f,\hat g]/\ii\hbar\) in quantum mechanics.
\(\Phi\), \(Q\)
Node flux (time integral of a node's voltage) and node charge: the "position" and "momentum" of a circuit.
\(\mathbf C\), \(\mathbf C^{-1}\)
The capacitance matrix of a layout and its inverse, which sets charging energies.
\(Z = \sqrt{L/C}\), \(Z_0\)
Impedance of an oscillator; characteristic impedance of a transmission line (usually 50 Ω).
\(R_Q = h/(2e)^2\)
The resistance quantum, about 6.45 kΩ. Circuits with impedance near it are strongly quantum.
\(\Phi_0 = h/2e\)
The superconducting flux quantum, \(2.07\times10^{-15}\) Wb.
\(Q_i\), \(Q_e\), \(Q_L\)
Internal, external (coupling) and loaded quality factors: \(1/Q_L = 1/Q_i + 1/Q_e\).
\(\kappa\)
An energy decay rate, \(\kappa = \omega/Q\). In optics, also a coupling per unit length.
\(g\), \(\Delta\)
Coupling strength and detuning between two oscillators; the dispersive shift is \(g^2/\Delta\).
\(\varepsilon_r\), \(\tan\delta\)
Relative permittivity and loss tangent of a dielectric.
participation ratio \(p\)
Fraction of a mode's electric energy stored in a given material; \(1/Q = \sum p\tan\delta\).
\(\lambda_L\), \(L_k\)
London penetration depth and kinetic inductance of a superconductor.
\(\Gamma\), \(S_{ij}\)
Reflection coefficient; scattering parameters of a microwave network.
\(n\), \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\), \(n_g\)
Refractive index, effective index of a waveguide mode, group index.
dB
Decibels: a power ratio of \(10^{x/10}\). Losses in dB add along a path.
\(k_B T\), \(\bar n\)
Thermal energy (about 20.8 GHz × \(h\) per kelvin) and the mean thermal photon number of a mode.
\(T_N\)
Noise temperature of an amplifier: the noise it adds, expressed as an equivalent resistor temperature.
