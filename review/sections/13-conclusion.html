Conclusion

Quantum hardware has acquired the institutions of a manufacturing industry (production lines, foundries, public capital and dated roadmaps) faster than it has acquired the design infrastructure that classical semiconductors built over three decades. The physics needed to fill that gap is well established: circuit quantisation and energy participation for superconducting devices, pseudopotentials for ion traps, loss budgets for photonics, cryogenic electrostatics for spins. So are the numerical and statistical methods: model-order reduction, domain decomposition, chance-constrained optimisation, hierarchical Bayesian models, experimental design and rare-event simulation. What is missing is their assembly into a flow that connects design intent to fabrication statistics and test data, and that scores a die by the quantity its buyer cares about, its logical error rate.

The junction's exponential sensitivity to barrier thickness, the exponential yield cliff of frequency collisions, and the quadratic dependence of error-correction overhead on \(1/\ln\Lambda\) all point the same way. Manufacturing quality, and the ability to predict it before a chip is cooled, is now the main lever on the cost of a logical qubit. The seven problems of §11 are a concrete agenda for pulling that lever, and they are aligned with the 2027–2029 milestones the industry has committed to in public.

Glossary of manufacturing and EDA terms

TermMeaning
EDAElectronic design automation: software for designing, simulating and verifying chips.
PDKProcess design kit: the foundry-supplied package of layer definitions, design rules, device models and parametrised cells that lets a designer target a process without knowing its recipe.
DRCDesign rule checking: verifying that a layout obeys geometric rules (widths, spacings, enclosures).
LVSLayout versus schematic: recognising devices in a layout and checking that the resulting netlist matches the intended circuit. "Layout versus Hamiltonian" in this review is the quantum analogue.
PEXParasitic extraction: computing the capacitances, resistances and inductances implied by a layout.
Sign-offThe set of checks a design must pass before a foundry accepts it for manufacture.
Tape-outRelease of a final design to the mask shop and fab.
SSTAStatistical static timing analysis: propagating process-variation distributions through a timing model, rather than checking fixed corners.
DFMDesign for manufacturability: design practices and tools that account for process variation and defects.
Compact modelA fast, physically based analytic model of a device (for example a SPICE transistor model) fitted to measurement.
Inline metrologyMeasurements made on wafers during fabrication (film thickness, critical dimensions, sheet resistance).
KGDKnown-good die: a die verified to meet specification before packaging.
TSVThrough-silicon via: a vertical conductor through the substrate.
Foundry, fablessA foundry manufactures chips designed by others; a fabless company designs chips and has them made by a foundry.
