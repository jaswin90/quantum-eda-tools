The industrial landscape, 2024–2026

Modalities and what is actually manufactured

The term "quantum chip" hides large differences in what is fabricated. For EDA the relevant question is which object has a layout, which physics maps that layout to a Hamiltonian, and who makes it. @tab:modalities summarises the answer for the six technologies with serious industrial backing.

Superconducting and spin-qubit processors are closest to classical integrated circuits: a layout is drawn, a process transfers it to a wafer, and the Hamiltonian follows from the geometry and the material parameters. Ion traps and photonic chips are also layout-defined, but the Hamiltonian is set by electrostatics and optics rather than by circuit quantisation. Neutral-atom qubits have no fabrication variation at all, since every atom is identical, and their manufacturing problems lie in optics and control. This partition determines where design automation can be sold, and §8 returns to it.

Vertical integration and the first quantum foundries

IBM and Anderon. IBM moved its processor wafers to the Albany NanoTech 300 mm facility in 2025. It says this doubles the speed of its research and development and permits ten-fold more complex chips, including the multilayer routing and long-range couplers of its Loon processor [@web_ibm_roadmap]. In 2026 IBM pledged more than US$10 billion over five years [@web_ibm_10b]. It also created Anderon, a standalone subsidiary operating a 300 mm quantum wafer fab. Anderon finalised a US$1 billion CHIPS Act award on 16 September 2026, which IBM matches with a further US$1 billion [@web_anderon]. Anderon is positioned as an open foundry: initially superconducting wafers and supporting electronics, with other modalities to follow. Its CEO has said that wafers could reach outside customers by the end of 2026 [@web_anderon_ieee]. As of November 2026 IBM is the only named customer; analysts name Rigetti, IQM and SEEQC as plausible candidates [@web_anderon_futurum].

IonQ. IonQ has pursued the most aggressive vertical integration. According to its quarterly filing it completed six acquisitions in 2025 with a combined purchase price of about US$2.66 billion, mostly in stock [@web_ionq_10q]. The technically decisive one was Oxford Ionics, whose traps drive gates with on-chip microwave and electronic fields rather than focused laser beams [@srinivas2021]; the others included Vector Atomic and Lightsynq. In January 2026 IonQ agreed to buy SkyWater Technology, a US pure-play foundry with fabs in Minnesota, Florida and Texas, for about US$1.8 billion in cash and stock. Trade press reports that the deal closed on 31 July 2026 [@web_ionq_skywater]. SkyWater continues to operate as a foundry under its own name. IonQ has since completed the purchase of Nexus Photonics, an integrated-photonics spin-out of UC Santa Barbara, and says it will offer photonic components to the wider industry through a quantum photonics foundry at SkyWater [@web_ionq_nexus; @web_forbes_ionq]. Qolab, a superconducting-qubit start-up co-founded by John Martinis, announced a commercial foundry collaboration with SkyWater in September 2026 [@web_qolab_skywater]. A competitor's designs will therefore be fabricated in a fab owned by IonQ.

Trapped ions without ownership. Quantinuum, listed on Nasdaq in June 2026 [@web_quantinuum_ipo], partners rather than buys. Infineon develops and manufactures its trap chips [@web_quantinuum_infineon], and NVIDIA processors run the real-time error-correction stack in its Helios system. Its roadmap moves from Helios to Sol (192 qubits, 2027) and Apollo (fault-tolerant, 2029). It also moves from linear race-track traps [@moses2023] to two-dimensional junctioned grids, which multiplies the electrode count.

Photonics on commercial lines. Photonic companies have used foundries from the outset. PsiQuantum's Omega chipset is made at GlobalFoundries [@psiquantum2025; @web_psiq]. Xanadu, listed by SPAC merger in March 2026 [@web_xanadu_ipo], expanded work with Tower Semiconductor in February 2026 [@web_xanadu_tower]. It announced wafer bonding with EV Group for heterogeneous materials in May 2026 [@web_xanadu_evg], and a multi-year programme on GlobalFoundries' 300 mm line for ultra-low-loss silicon nitride and superconducting nanowire detectors in October 2026 [@web_xanadu_gf]. Quandela's quantum-dot sources remain in-house [@web_quandela].

Silicon spins and the Classiq acquisition. Intel fabricates spin qubits on its 300 mm lines and co-developed a 300 mm cryogenic wafer prober with Bluefors and Afore [@neyens2024; @web_bluefors]. On 26 October 2026 it agreed to acquire Classiq, an Israeli quantum-software company whose product synthesises optimised circuits from high-level algorithm descriptions. The price is not disclosed; press reports put it at about US$2.3 billion, and closing is expected in the first half of 2027 [@web_intel_classiq]. Intel Capital also led QuantWare's US$178 million Series B in May 2026 [@web_quantware]. QuantWare sells superconducting processors to other system builders, is building a dedicated fab ("KiloFab") in Delft, and plans first shipments of its 10,000-qubit VIO-40K architecture in 2028.

Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA. Google remains fully integrated. It took in the MIT spin-out Atlantic Quantum, whose fluxonium qubits and modular stack broaden its superconducting roadmap [@web_google_aq], and in March 2026 it added a neutral-atom programme alongside superconducting work [@web_google_atoms]. Microsoft's Majorana 2 device, announced in June 2026, is reported by the company to have parity lifetimes of order seconds [@web_msft_maj2]. The topological interpretation remains contested, and DARPA now has on-site access for independent testing [@web_msft_darpa]. Microsoft hedges through Atom Computing and Quantinuum. NVIDIA builds no qubits but is present in nearly every stack. Its CUDA-Q platform and NVQLink interconnect (17 processor builders at launch) put GPUs inside the control loop, and in March 2026 it added microsecond-latency real-time decoding [@web_nvqlink; @web_nvidia_gtc26]. NVIDIA's venture arm holds stakes in Quantinuum, PsiQuantum, QuEra, Alice & Bob and Riverlane [@web_alicebob_nv].

Validation. DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative advanced Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ to its final Stage C in October 2026, joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum, which entered through the predecessor programme [@web_darpa_qbi]. Stage C is independent verification of hardware and system designs. It rewards teams with a documented, credible path to manufacture.

Reading the pattern

Capital has flowed to the two ends of the stack. At the bottom, companies have secured wafer capacity: IonQ–SkyWater, IBM–Anderon, QuantWare's KiloFab, Xanadu on GlobalFoundries. At the top, they have secured the software users touch: Intel–Classiq, NVIDIA's CUDA-Q, and the control and calibration vendors. Neither acquisitions nor venture rounds have targeted the layer that classical semiconductors call EDA: the tools that turn design intent into a manufacturable layout and verify that it will work. Quarterly venture tables for 2026 list large rounds for quantum hardware makers and for AI-driven classical chip design, and none that we could identify as quantum design automation [@web_semieng_q3]. That is an absence of evidence from public sources, not proof of absence. It is, however, consistent with the technical survey in §3–§7, which finds that the design layer is still largely made up of open-source research tools and in-house scripts.
