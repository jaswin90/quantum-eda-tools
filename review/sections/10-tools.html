The existing tool landscape

Incumbent EDA and simulation vendors

Keysight is the only incumbent with a product built for qubits. QuantumPro, launched on its ADS platform in 2024, integrates schematic, layout, electromagnetic analysis, nonlinear circuit simulation and quantum-parameter extraction for superconducting devices [@web_keysight_qp]. Quantum System Analysis, added in October 2025, extends this to system-level simulation and is described as extensible to spin qubits [@web_keysight_qsa]. Keysight also sells control hardware, so it spans design and test. It is the incumbent most likely to move into sign-off, and the most credible partner or acquirer.

Synopsys, which completed its acquisition of Ansys in 2025, owns the solvers most qubit designers already use (HFSS, Q3D, Lumerical for photonics), plus QuantumATK for atomistic modelling and Sentaurus for device simulation. It has worked with Qolab and Applied Materials on qubit materials and processes [@web_synopsys_q]. Its May 2026 collaboration with Quantinuum concerns quantum algorithms for engineering simulation [@web_synopsys_qtnm]: Synopsys as a user of quantum computers, not a supplier of tools for building them. Cadence brings the classical analogue and mixed-signal flow (Virtuoso, Spectre, Innovus, Pegasus, Clarity 3D). That flow applies directly to spin qubits and cryogenic control, as the Equal1 case and the cryogenic 22FDX models show [@web_cadence_equal1; @web_cadence_cryo]. Siemens EDA (Calibre sign-off, L-Edit layout) is widely used on non-standard processes, but we found no quantum-specific offering. COMSOL is the default multiphysics solver for traps, packages and thermal design.

Fabrication and test equipment

Qubits made on 300 mm lines use the same deposition, etch and lithography equipment as classical chips. The software layer on top of that equipment, above all the metrology-to-yield analytics that classical fabs buy from KLA, has no quantum version. Applied Materials is the active equipment maker. It has invested in Qolab and co-authored a roadmap on scaling superconducting qubits, with junction uniformity as the stated aim [@web_qolab_b; @web_synopsys_q]. We found no public quantum-specific product or partnership from Lam Research or KLA. That is an absence in public sources, not evidence of no internal work. EV Group and DISCO supply bonding and dicing to Xanadu [@web_xanadu_evg]. Bluefors, Afore and FormFactor sell cryogenic probers [@web_bluefors].

Quantum-native tools

On the design side almost everything is open source, written by hardware groups for their own use: Quantum Metal, KQCircuits, gdsfactory [@gdsfactory], Palace, pyEPR, scqubits, QuCAT, SQcircuit, SQuADDS and SQDMetal (@sec:design). These are good tools and any product should interoperate with them. But Quantum Metal's packaged release is frozen at a pre-0.5 version, with newer versions installed from source [@quantummetal], and a foundry cannot ask paying customers to sign off with unsupported code. Funded companies cluster at the two ends. At the device end there is Nanoacademic's QTCAD for spin-qubit simulation, whose version 2.0 shipped in 2026 [@web_qtcad]. At the operating end there are control vendors (Quantum Machines, Qblox, Zurich Instruments), calibration firms (Q-CTRL, Qruise, QuantrolOx), error correction (Riverlane) and algorithm-level synthesis (Classiq, being acquired by Intel). NVIDIA's CUDA-Q and Google's Stim are the de facto open standards for hybrid execution and QEC simulation [@web_nvidia_gtc26; @gidney2021stim].

The gap map

The three large openings (sign-off, yield and the test-data layer) are coupled. Sign-off needs rules and variation models; these come from yield learning; and yield learning needs test data joined to design and process history. Whoever owns all three owns the feedback loop between design and fabrication.
