Other modalities through the same lens

For each modality we ask the same three questions as for superconducting circuits: what maps layout to Hamiltonian, what varies in manufacture, and what is missing from the tool chain.

Trapped ions

A surface-electrode trap holds ions tens of microns above a chip [@chiaverini2005]. A radio-frequency drive at \(\Omega\) produces a time-averaged pseudopotential, and static voltages \(V_k\) on control electrodes position and move the ions [@leibfried2003]:

$$ \Psi(\mathbf r) = \frac{q^2\,|\mathbf E_{\mathrm{rf}}(\mathbf r)|^2}{4m\Omega^2}, \qquad \phi(\mathbf r,t) = \sum_k V_k(t)\,\phi_k(\mathbf r), \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{sec}} \approx \frac{q_M\Omega}{2\sqrt2}\ \ (a_M = 0), $$

where \(\phi_k\) is the basis potential of electrode \(k\) at unit voltage and \(q_M\) the Mathieu parameter. Because the problem is linear in \(V_k\), transport waveforms follow from regularised least squares,

$$ \min_{\mathbf V(t)} \ \big\|\mathbf A\mathbf V(t) - \mathbf b(t)\big\|^2 + \lambda\big\|\dot{\mathbf V}(t)\big\|^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad |V_k|\le V_{\max}, $$

with \(\mathbf A\) collecting the basis potentials and their derivatives at the target ion positions. House's analytic model of planar electrodes gives a fast and accurate approximation to \(\phi_k\) [@house2008], a ready-made surrogate. Electric-field noise from the surface heats the ion's motion at a rate that scales roughly as \(d^{-4}\) with ion–surface distance [@brownnutt2015], which sets a floor on trap size.

The industrial change is scale and ownership. The quantum CCD architecture moves ions between zones [@kielpinski2002; @pino2021; @moses2023]. Two-dimensional junctioned traps for Quantinuum's Sol and Apollo, made by Infineon, need hundreds to thousands of electrodes with multilayer routing beneath the surface [@bruzewicz2019]. Integrated waveguides for addressing [@mehta2020; @niffenegger2020] add photonic design rules. Microwave and electronic gates [@ospelkaus2011; @srinivas2021], the basis of Oxford Ionics and now IonQ, turn the trap into something much closer to a conventional mixed-signal chip, with current-carrying structures whose near fields set gate fidelity. The missing tools are parametrised electrode cells, automatic routing of electrode lines, rule decks for trap processes at Infineon and SkyWater, and optimisation of geometry and waveforms together (Problem 7 in §11).

Photonics

In photonic architectures the dominant error is a lost photon. Transmissions multiply along a path \(\mathcal P\), so losses in decibels add: \(\eta_{\mathcal P} = \prod_{i\in\mathcal P}\eta_i = 10^{-\sum_i\ell_i/10}\). For continuous-variable architectures based on Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) states [@gottesman2001; @bourassa2021], loss replaces part of a squeezed quadrature with vacuum noise. In shot-noise units,

$$ V_{\mathrm{out}} = \eta\,V_{\mathrm{in}} + (1-\eta), \qquad S_{\mathrm{out}} = -10\log_{10}V_{\mathrm{out}}\ \ \mathrm{dB}, $$

which caps the attainable squeezing at \(-10\log_{10}(1-\eta)\) regardless of source quality. Taking the roughly 10 dB regime targeted by published blueprints as a proxy requirement, a 15 dB source tolerates only about 7% total loss from source to detector (@fig:squeezing). For fusion-based schemes with single photons [@bartolucci2023; @rudolph2017], thresholds on photon loss per fusion are likewise percent-level. Either way, each of the many components on a path is held to fractions of a percent. Demonstrations include Xanadu's Borealis and Aurora [@madsen2022; @aghaeerad2025] and PsiQuantum's Omega platform [@psiquantum2025]; the broader field is reviewed in [@wang2020photonics].

Photonic design automation is mature for classical silicon photonics, with component solvers, circuit simulators and foundry kits [@bogaerts2018]. Quantum use differs in its statistical demands. The requirement is \(\Pr\big(\min_{\mathcal P}\eta_{\mathcal P}\ge\eta_{\min}\big)\ge Y_{\min}\) over the foundry's measured component-loss distributions, verified for every path in a circuit of thousands of components. Heterogeneous integration (lithium niobate modulators, superconducting nanowire detectors) also falls outside standard kits. Quandela's approach differs again: deterministic quantum-dot sources [@somaschi2016; @maring2024] whose spectral inhomogeneity makes source selection and matching a yield problem.

Silicon spin qubits

A spin qubit is a single electron or hole confined beneath gate electrodes [@loss1998; @burkard2023]. Its design is cryogenic electrostatics: the coupled Poisson and Schrödinger problem

$$ -\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\varepsilon\nabla\phi\big) = \rho[\phi], \qquad \Big[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^*}\nabla^2 - e\phi(\mathbf r)\Big]\psi = E\psi, $$

solved at temperatures where conventional device simulators fail to converge. Nanoacademic's QTCAD was built for this [@beaudoin2022; @web_qtcad]. The qubit frequency is \(hf = g\mu_BB\), about 28 GHz per tesla for \(g\approx2\). Two-qubit gates use exchange, \(J\approx 4t^2/U\), with tunnel coupling \(t\) exponential in the barrier-gate voltage. That exponential is the weakness: low-frequency charge noise with spectrum \(S_V(f)\propto1/f\) maps to gate error through \(\partial J/\partial V\). The manufacturing advantage is compatibility with CMOS lines [@zwerver2022; @vandersypen2017], and fidelities have crossed surface-code thresholds [@xue2022]. The open problem is variability: sharing control lines across many dots requires threshold voltages uniform to well below the charging energy. Wafer-scale cryogenic data [@neyens2024] are exactly what a compact model of a quantum dot needs, in the way SPICE models are fitted to transistor data [@nagel1975]. No such model exists in any PDK. Classical EDA fits spin qubits best, and the opportunity there is to complement Cadence and Synopsys, not to replace them.

Neutral atoms

Neutral-atom processors hold atoms in optical tweezers and entangle them through Rydberg interactions [@saffman2010]. Gate fidelities above 99.5% [@evered2023], coherent transport of entangled arrays [@bluvstein2022] and logical-level algorithms on up to 48 logical qubits [@bluvstein2024] have been shown. Because atoms are identical, there is no qubit fabrication variation, and the layout-to-Hamiltonian tool chain of §3–§4 does not apply to the qubits. Two openings remain. Reconfigurable connectivity makes neutral atoms natural hosts for qLDPC codes [@xu2024], so the QEC tooling of §9 applies fully. And the optics that steer the tweezers are migrating onto photonic integrated circuits, which do need design and verification.

Topological qubits

Majorana-based qubits would store information non-locally and protect it topologically [@nayak2008]. Microsoft's devices pass a "topological gap protocol" [@aghaee2023] and have shown interferometric single-shot parity measurement [@msft2025]. The company reports parity lifetimes of order seconds for Majorana 2 [@web_msft_maj2]. Whether these devices realise topological protection remains disputed, and DARPA's on-site evaluation is under way [@web_msft_darpa]. For design automation this is a late-stage account. If validated, gate-defined nanowire devices would need the same cryogenic electrostatic simulation as spin qubits.
